More Than a Century of Hockey

The National Hockey League is one of the most historic sports leagues in the world. Its history is filled with legendary players, unforgettable rivalries, championship dynasties and moments that changed the sport forever.

This museum explores the teams, players and events that made the NHL what it is today.

📜 The NHL Historical Timeline

1895- The pre era of hockey.

1917 – The Birth of the NHL

The National Hockey League is founded in Montreal on November 26, 1917. The league begins with four founding clubs.

1918 – The First NHL Champion

Toronto Arenas win the first Stanley Cup championship awarded during NHL competition.

1924 – The Boston Bruins Arrive

Boston becomes the first American-based NHL franchise.

1926 – The League Expands

The New York Rangers, Chicago Black Hawks and Detroit Cougars join the NHL.

1942 – The Original Six Era

The league enters its famous six-team period, which lasts until the 1967 expansion.

1950s – Montreal's Great Dynasty

The Canadiens become the dominant force in hockey, winning five consecutive Cups from 1956 through 1960.

1967 – A Massive Expansion

The NHL doubles in size from six to twelve franchises.

1970s – The Broad Street Bullies

Philadelphia wins consecutive championships in 1974 and 1975.

1980s – Gretzky Changes Everything

Wayne Gretzky transforms offensive hockey and leads Edmonton to multiple championships.

1990s – The NHL Goes Global

More international talent enters the league. Superstars like Mario Lemieux, Jaromir Jagr and Dominik Hasek define the decade.

2004–2005 – The Lockout

A labour dispute results in the cancellation of the entire 2004–05 NHL season.

2010s – The Modern Dynasties

Chicago, Los Angeles and Pittsburgh establish championship-winning teams.

2020s – A New Generation

Connor McDavid, Nathan MacKinnon and other stars lead the modern NHL.

⭐ The Original Six

Six historic franchises competed in every NHL season from 1942–43 through 1966–67.

🐻 Boston Bruins

Founded in 1924, the Bruins became the first American NHL franchise.

Legends: Bobby Orr, Ray Bourque, Phil Esposito.

🖤 Chicago Blackhawks

Established in 1926, Chicago became one of hockey's iconic original franchises.

Legends: Bobby Hull, Stan Mikita, Patrick Kane.

🔴 Detroit Red Wings

Founded in 1926, Detroit earned the nickname Hockeytown.

Legends: Gordie Howe, Steve Yzerman, Nicklas Lidstrom.

🔵 Montreal Canadiens

Founded in 1909, Montreal joined the NHL in its inaugural season.

Legends: Maurice Richard, Jean Beliveau, Guy Lafleur.

🗽 New York Rangers

Established in 1926, the Rangers became a major part of New York sports history.

Legends: Mark Messier, Brian Leetch, Henrik Lundqvist.

🍁 Toronto Maple Leafs

Toronto's NHL history began with the Arenas before the Maple Leafs name was adopted in 1927.

Legends: Syl Apps, Dave Keon, Mats Sundin.

🏆 THE STANLEY CUP CHAMPIONSHIP ARCHIVE

The Stanley Cup is the oldest trophy competed for by professional athletes in North America. It was donated by Lord Stanley in 1892.

The archive below includes NHL-era championship seasons, plus the historical 1919 cancellation.

👑 THE SUPERSTARS OF EVERY DECADE

1920s — Howie Morenz

The Montreal Canadiens legend became one of the NHL's first major attractions. His speed and offensive ability made him a household name among hockey fans.

1930s — Eddie Shore

A legendary Boston Bruins defenseman. Shore was famous for his physical play, competitive attitude and defensive excellence.

1940s — Maurice Richard

Known as The Rocket, Richard became the first player to score 50 goals in 50 games. He became a symbol of Montreal hockey.

1950s — Gordie Howe

Mr. Hockey was an incredibly complete player. He combined scoring, toughness and longevity in a way few players have ever matched.

1960s — Bobby Orr

Orr transformed the defense position. His skating, passing and offensive instincts changed how hockey teams used defensemen.

1970s — Guy Lafleur

The Flower became one of the most exciting offensive players of the decade. His speed and scoring helped Montreal dominate the late 1970s.

1980s — Wayne Gretzky

The Great One dominated scoring records. He recorded 200 points in a season four times and led Edmonton's historic dynasty.

1990s — Mario Lemieux

Super Mario was a phenomenal scorer. His combination of size, hands and vision made him one of hockey's greatest talents.

2000s — Sidney Crosby

Crosby arrived in 2005 and quickly became one of the league's most important players. He won the Stanley Cup in 2009.

2010s — Sidney Crosby

Crosby led Pittsburgh to three championships and became one of the defining players of the modern NHL.

2020s — Connor McDavid

McDavid's skating, creativity and scoring have made him one of the most electrifying players of his generation.

These are editorial selections representing each era, not official NHL decade awards.

💥 UNFORGETTABLE NHL MOMENTS

1955 — The Richard Riot

Maurice Richard's suspension sparked major unrest in Montreal. The event became a significant moment in Quebec's sporting and cultural history.

1972 — The Summit Series

Canada faced the Soviet Union in an eight-game series that became one of the most important international hockey events ever played.

1980 — The Miracle on Ice

The United States Olympic team defeated the Soviet Union in a historic upset at the Winter Olympics.

1988 — Gretzky Leaves Edmonton

Wayne Gretzky was traded to the Los Angeles Kings in one of the most famous trades in sports history.

1994 — Rangers End the Drought

The New York Rangers captured the Stanley Cup, ending a championship drought that stretched back to 1940.

2010 — The Golden Goal

Sidney Crosby scored the overtime winner against the United States to give Canada Olympic gold in Vancouver.

2019 — St. Louis Wins Its First Cup

The Blues captured their first Stanley Cup after decades of waiting.

2024 — Florida's First Championship

The Florida Panthers won their first Stanley Cup after reaching the Final in consecutive seasons.

🌎 HOW THE NHL EXPANDED

1917 — The NHL Begins

The NHL begins with four founding clubs: Montreal Canadiens, Montreal Wanderers, Ottawa Senators and Quebec Bulldogs.

1924 — Boston Joins

The Boston Bruins become the first American-based NHL franchise.

1926 — Major American Expansion

The New York Rangers, Chicago Black Hawks and Detroit Cougars join the league.

1967 — The Historic Expansion

The NHL doubles from six teams to twelve. New franchises include Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Los Angeles, Minnesota, Oakland and St. Louis.

1970–1979 — A Bigger League

Buffalo, Vancouver, Atlanta, Washington, Kansas City and Colorado's original Rockies franchise enter during this era.

1991–1993 — The Southern Expansion

San Jose, Ottawa, Tampa Bay and Florida help transform the league's geographic reach.

1998–2000 — More New Franchises

Nashville, Atlanta and Minnesota enter the NHL during this expansion period.

2017 — Vegas Golden Knights

Vegas becomes the league's 31st team and reaches the Stanley Cup Final in its inaugural season.

2021 — Seattle Kraken

Seattle joins the NHL as the 32nd franchise. The league enters its current 32-team era.

🏅 THE NHL'S MOST FAMOUS AWARDS

Hart Memorial Trophy

Awarded to the player judged most valuable to his team during the regular season.

Art Ross Trophy

Awarded to the player who leads the league in regular-season points.

Rocket Richard Trophy

Awarded to the NHL's regular-season leading goal scorer.

Vezina Trophy

Awarded to the NHL's top goaltender, as voted by the league's general managers.

Norris Trophy

Awarded to the defenseman who demonstrates the greatest all-around ability.

Calder Trophy

Awarded to the NHL's top rookie.

Conn Smythe Trophy

Awarded to the most valuable player during the Stanley Cup playoffs.

Selke Trophy

Awarded to the forward who demonstrates the greatest defensive ability.

Lady Byng Trophy

Recognizes sportsmanship and gentlemanly conduct combined with a high standard of playing ability.

Jack Adams Award

Awarded to the NHL's coach of the year.

🏆 THE LEGACY OF THE NHL

Over a Century of Greatness

The NHL began as a small Canadian league in 1917 and grew into an international professional hockey competition.

Generations of players have transformed the sport. From Maurice Richard and Gordie Howe to Wayne Gretzky, Mario Lemieux, Sidney Crosby and Connor McDavid, every era has produced unforgettable talent.

The Stanley Cup remains the ultimate prize for NHL teams, representing the culmination of an entire season of competition.
