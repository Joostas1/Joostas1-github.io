1. The Beginning of the NHL (1917)

The National Hockey League, also known as the NHL, was officially founded on November 26, 1917, in Montreal, Quebec, Canada.

The NHL was created after disagreements within the National Hockey Association (NHA). The original meeting involved representatives from Montreal, Ottawa and Quebec.

The league officially began playing games on December 19, 1917.

The Original Founding Clubs

  • Montreal Canadiens
  • Montreal Wanderers
  • Ottawa Senators
  • Quebec Bulldogs

The Toronto Arenas were added as a fifth club. The Quebec Bulldogs did not operate during the first season, so four teams actually played.

2. The Early Years (1918–1942)

During the early years, professional hockey was very different from today's game. The league had fewer teams, smaller arenas, different equipment and fewer rules.

1918

The Montreal Wanderers stopped playing after their arena was destroyed by fire.

1919

The Stanley Cup Final between Montreal and Seattle was abandoned during the influenza pandemic.

1924

The Boston Bruins became the NHL's first American-based team.

1926

The New York Rangers, Chicago Black Hawks and Detroit Cougars joined the NHL.

1927

The NHL took control of the Stanley Cup competition.

1933

The NHL held its first All-Star benefit game in honour of injured player Ace Bailey.

1937

The Detroit franchise became known as the Detroit Red Wings.

1942

The Brooklyn Americans stopped playing. The league was reduced to six teams.

3. The Original Six Era (1942–1967)

The Original Six era lasted 25 seasons. Only six teams competed in the NHL during this period.

The Six Original Six Teams

  • Boston Bruins
  • Chicago Black Hawks
  • Detroit Red Wings
  • Montreal Canadiens
  • New York Rangers
  • Toronto Maple Leafs

This period helped establish some of hockey's greatest rivalries and most famous players. The Montreal Canadiens and Toronto Maple Leafs were especially successful.

Important Developments

  • Television broadcasts helped hockey reach more fans.
  • The NHL developed its award and trophy system.
  • The Hockey Hall of Fame opened in 1961.
  • Scouting and player development became more organized.
  • The Stanley Cup playoffs became a major sporting event.

4. NHL Expansion (1967–1990)

In 1967, the NHL doubled in size from six teams to twelve teams. This was one of the biggest changes in professional hockey history.

The Six Expansion Teams of 1967

  • California Seals
  • Los Angeles Kings
  • Minnesota North Stars
  • Philadelphia Flyers
  • Pittsburgh Penguins
  • St. Louis Blues

Major Expansion Years

1970

Buffalo Sabres and Vancouver Canucks joined.

1972

The World Hockey Association (WHA) was founded, creating competition for NHL players.

1974

Washington Capitals and Kansas City Scouts joined.

1976

The California franchise moved to Cleveland and became the Cleveland Barons.

1979

Four WHA teams joined the NHL: Edmonton Oilers, Hartford Whalers, Quebec Nordiques and Winnipeg Jets.

1980

The Atlanta Flames moved to Calgary and became the Calgary Flames.

1982

The Colorado Rockies moved to New Jersey and became the New Jersey Devils.

5. The NHL During the 1990s

The 1990s brought major changes to the NHL. New teams joined, older franchises relocated, and the league expanded across North America.

  • 1991: San Jose Sharks joined.
  • 1992: Ottawa Senators and Tampa Bay Lightning joined.
  • 1993: Florida Panthers and Mighty Ducks of Anaheim joined.
  • 1993: Minnesota North Stars relocated to Dallas.
  • 1995: Quebec Nordiques relocated to Denver and became the Colorado Avalanche.
  • 1996: Winnipeg Jets relocated to Phoenix.
  • 1997: Hartford Whalers relocated to North Carolina and became the Carolina Hurricanes.
  • 1998: Nashville Predators joined.
  • 1999: Atlanta Thrashers joined.

By the end of the decade, the NHL was approaching 30 teams.

6. The Modern NHL (2000–2026)

2000

Columbus Blue Jackets and Minnesota Wild joined. The NHL reached 30 teams.

2003

The first modern NHL regular-season outdoor game was played in Edmonton. It was called the Heritage Classic.

2004–2005

A labour dispute caused the cancellation of the entire NHL season.

2008

The first Winter Classic was played between Buffalo and Pittsburgh.

2011

The Atlanta Thrashers moved to Winnipeg and became the second Winnipeg Jets franchise.

2013

Another lockout shortened the NHL season to 48 games.

2017

The Vegas Golden Knights joined the league.

2020

The COVID-19 pandemic interrupted the season. The NHL later completed a modified playoff format.

2021

The Seattle Kraken joined the NHL, bringing the league to 32 teams.

2024

Utah received an NHL franchise through the transfer of the Arizona Coyotes' hockey operations and roster.

2025–2026

The Utah franchise became the Utah Mammoth. The NHL continued competing with 32 teams.

9. The Stanley Cup

The Stanley Cup is the championship trophy awarded to the NHL playoff champion. It is one of the oldest and most recognizable trophies in professional sports.

The trophy was donated by Lord Stanley of Preston in 1892 and first awarded in 1893.

Important Stanley Cup Facts

  • The Montreal Canadiens have won the most Stanley Cups in NHL history, with 24 championships.
  • The Toronto Maple Leafs last won in 1967.
  • The Edmonton Oilers won five championships during the 1980s.
  • The Pittsburgh Penguins became a major championship team in the modern era.
  • The Vegas Golden Knights won their first Stanley Cup in 2023.
  • The Florida Panthers won their first Stanley Cup in 2024.

12. Conclusion

The National Hockey League has grown tremendously since its founding in 1917.

What started as a small Canadian professional hockey league has become one of the world's most recognized sports leagues.

From the Original Six to modern superstars, the NHL has created unforgettable moments, historic rivalries and legendary championships.

The history of the NHL is more than 100 years of hockey, competition, dedication and tradition.
